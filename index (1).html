What we offer, right now

Ways to take part today

Voice
A place to say what you actually think. Essays, opinion pieces, and open discussion on the global issues members care about — written for an audience of peers, not graded by a teacher.
Talents
Not everything worth saying is an essay. Poems, writing, art, photography, and other creative work that responds to the same questions — a showcase for talent that wouldn't otherwise find an audience.
Research
Real inquiry, led by teens. Members dig into global issues properly — sources, data, context — and present what they find, building research skills years before university asks them to.
Language Access
Ideas shouldn't stop at a language barrier. Members translate and adapt each other's work so it reaches more readers, and so no one's fluency decides whether their thinking gets heard.

About Eleftheria

Freedom to be heard, wherever you're speaking from.

Most teenagers who care about the state of the world don't have anywhere serious to put that concern. School doesn't always leave room for it. The news treats them as an audience, not a participant. Eleftheria exists to close that gap — a space where young people can research, write, and speak about the issues shaping their generation, in their own words and, whenever possible, their own language.

We're not a classroom and we're not a campaign. We're a community: teenagers from different countries, school systems, and circumstances, working out loud on the things they can't stop thinking about — and getting better at thinking about them together.

Our name comes from the Greek word for freedom. It's a reminder of what the community is for: the freedom to think seriously about the world before anyone's asked you to, and to say what you find in more than one language.

Peer-led

Members set the agenda. Nothing here is assigned, graded, or edited to sound older than it is.

Multilingual by default

Work gets translated, not just published — so ideas travel as far as the people who wrote them.

Global, on purpose

Members from different countries compare notes on the same issue — that comparison is the point.
